What makes a result worth trusting? These projects explore that question through corporate disclosures, controlled machine learning experiments, and decisions under uncertainty.

    <p class="resource-index">01 / Financial text analysis</p>
    <h2>TextQuant</h2>
    <p>A research prototype that flags corporate sustainability claims missing the scope, baseline, or method needed to verify them. I compared a decision tree, document-level statistics, and a rubric-guided language model, and tested where those methods disagreed.</p>
    <p><a href="https://github.com/Angelinaaa77/hackmit-greenwash-audit#readme">Read the TextQuant methodology and results</a></p>

  <section class="recruiter-resource">
    <p class="resource-index">02 / Experimental machine learning</p>
    <h2>Neural networks from scratch</h2>
    <p>A NumPy implementation of backpropagation, trained on MNIST. I compared eight learning rates from the same initialization to isolate their effect; the best runs reached 92.2% test accuracy after ten epochs.</p>
    <p><a href="https://github.com/Angelinaaa77/Neural-Network-Backpropagation#readme">See the learning-rate experiment and code</a></p>
  </section>
  <section class="recruiter-resource">
    <p class="resource-index">03 / Sequential decisions</p>
    <h2>Q-learning vs. SARSA</h2>
    <p>A controlled GridWorld study of how exploration affects learned policies. Both algorithms found six-step routes, but SARSA learned a path that stayed farther from a costly state.</p>
    <p><a href="https://github.com/Angelinaaa77/Q-learning-SARSA-GridWorld#readme">Compare the policies and value maps</a></p>
